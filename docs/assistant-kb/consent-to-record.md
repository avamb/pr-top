# Consent to Record: What Therapists Need to Know Before Uploading a Session

PR-TOP lets you upload audio or video recordings of therapy sessions for
transcription and AI-assisted note generation. Before you upload any recording,
you must obtain the client's **prior, explicit consent**. This page explains
the legal basis, what consent wording should cover, ready-to-use templates in
four languages, and how the recording-consent flag works in PR-TOP.

## Why Consent to Record Is Legally Required

Recording a therapy session captures sensitive health data about the client.
Under GDPR (and equivalent laws in non-EU countries), health data is
**special-category data** requiring a higher bar for lawful processing.

Recording without consent may result in:
- A breach of your professional ethics code (most national psychological
  associations list it as a disciplinary matter)
- A GDPR infringement report to the supervisory authority
- Civil liability toward the client
- In some jurisdictions, a criminal offence under wiretapping or privacy laws

Obtaining written consent **before the first recorded session** is the standard
safest practice across the EU, UK, and most other countries where PR-TOP is used.

## Legal Basis Under GDPR (EU/EEA Therapists)

Relevant articles:

- **Article 9(1)** — Prohibits processing of health data by default.
- **Article 9(2)(a)** — Allows it with the data subject's **explicit consent**
  for one or more specified purposes.
- **Article 9(2)(h)** — Allows it for health-care purposes under an obligation
  of professional secrecy, subject to national law.

In practice, most EU member states (Germany, France, Netherlands, Austria, the
Baltic states) require explicit written consent for recording rather than
relying on the professional-secrecy exception. Always check your national
supervisory authority's guidance. The Estonian GDPR implementation (the
primary jurisdiction for PR-TOP's operator) follows the Article 9(2)(a)
explicit-consent route for clinical audio recordings.

**Important:** PR-TOP processes recordings as a data processor under your
instructions. The legal responsibility for obtaining and documenting consent
rests with you as the data controller.

## Prerequisites Before Uploading Any Recording

1. Informed consent obtained and signed before the session was recorded.
2. Consent document stored securely in your own records (paper file or a
   dedicated consent-management system — not in PR-TOP).
3. 'Recording consent granted' flag set to **Yes** on the client's profile
   in PR-TOP (Client profile → context tab or client detail header).
4. Consent covers the specific purpose: producing session notes and summaries,
   and (if applicable) video.

If the client has **not** consented to recording, use the
**single-track / keep-only-my-voice** option. This strips the client's voice
from the upload so only your voice is transcribed, and the client's audio is
discarded server-side before any transcript is stored.

## Step-by-Step: Getting Consent Right

1. **Explain purpose and scope.** Tell the client that sessions may be
   recorded, that the recording is used solely to produce session notes and
   summaries, that no third party receives the recording, and that they can
   withdraw consent at any time.

2. **Provide the consent form in writing.** Use one of the templates below,
   adapted to your jurisdiction. The form should be in a language the client
   reads fluently.

3. **Obtain a signed copy.** Keep it in your paper file or consent-management
   system. Do not upload the consent form to PR-TOP.

4. **Set the flag in PR-TOP.** Open the client's profile and confirm that the
   'Recording consent' indicator is set to Yes. This is a manual step — PR-TOP
   does not automatically link the flag to any document.

5. **Before each recorded session**, verbally confirm that consent still stands.
   If the client withdraws consent, uncheck the flag immediately and do not
   upload any new recording. You may delete existing recordings from the
   session detail page if the client requests erasure.

## Video-Specific Considerations

If you record video (not just audio), the consent wording must **explicitly
cover video**. Video captures biometric data (facial images, body language)
in addition to the spoken content, which some supervisory authorities treat as
requiring a separate, explicit consent clause. In practice:

- Use "audio and/or video" in the consent wording (the templates above already
  do this).
- If you are only recording audio, you may remove the "video" reference to
  keep the form precise.
- Some jurisdictions (e.g., Germany in certain Länder, and France for
  telehealth providers) publish specific guidance on video-session consent —
  always check your local professional association's guidelines.

## Storing and Managing Consent Documents

PR-TOP stores **only a boolean flag** — "Recording consent granted: Yes / No"
— on the client profile. PR-TOP does not store, index, or process consent
documents. You are responsible for:

1. **Keeping the signed consent form** in your own secure records (paper file,
   a dedicated practice-management system, or an encrypted document store).
2. **Updating the flag in PR-TOP** promptly if consent is withdrawn.
3. **Linking consent to the correct client** — if you use a pseudonymised
   paper file, ensure you can trace the signed form to the PR-TOP client ID.

There is no expiry built into the flag. If you work with clients over many
years, consider a policy of re-confirming consent annually, especially if the
scope of recording changes (e.g., switching from audio to video sessions).

## Edge Cases and Troubleshooting

**The client consented verbally but has not signed the form.**
Verbal consent is not sufficient under GDPR Article 9(2)(a) for health data.
Do not upload the recording until you have a signed form. Schedule a brief
admin moment at the start of the next session to obtain the signature.

**The client previously consented but has now asked that recordings stop.**
Unset the 'Recording consent' flag in PR-TOP immediately. Do not upload any
recordings made after the withdrawal date. For recordings already uploaded, the
client may exercise the right to erasure (GDPR Article 17) — delete the
relevant session recording, transcript, and summary from the session detail page.

**I already uploaded a recording before getting consent.**
Delete the session recording immediately from the session detail page. Explain
to the client what happened, obtain proper consent, and re-record or proceed
without a recording. Note the incident in your supervision log.

**The client is a minor.**
Parental or guardian consent is required in addition to (not instead of)
age-appropriate assent from the child. Consult your professional association's
guidelines on recording sessions with minors.

**Telehealth / video calls — can I use the platform's own recording?**
If your video platform (Zoom, Teams, etc.) records the session, that is a
separate recording processed by the platform provider. Download the file and
upload it to PR-TOP as a session recording. The same consent requirement
applies — but you also need to comply with the video platform's own
data-processing terms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a separate consent form for each client?**
Yes. Consent is per data-subject. Each client must sign their own form before
their first recorded session.

**Can consent be given by email or text message?**
A text or email can constitute written consent if it is sufficiently explicit
and stored securely. However, a signed paper or PDF form is easier to produce
as evidence if challenged. Prefer the formal template.

**What if my national law is stricter than GDPR?**
National law may add requirements (e.g., notarised signatures, specific
retention limits, mandatory clauses). Always apply the stricter standard.
The templates above are a minimum baseline.

**Does PR-TOP verify that consent was obtained before upload?**
PR-TOP checks the 'Recording consent' flag on the client profile before
allowing an upload. If the flag is set to No, the upload button is disabled.
However, PR-TOP cannot verify that a consent document actually exists — that
responsibility remains with you.

**Can I record a session with the single-track option if the client has not
consented?**
Yes. The single-track option extracts only your voice and discards the
client's audio server-side before any transcript is stored. You can upload such
a recording even if the 'Recording consent' flag is No, because the client's
voice is never retained.
